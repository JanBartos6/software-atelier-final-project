The Rise of Industrial Robots: Transforming Modern Factories

Industrial robots are programmable machines designed to perform tasks in manufacturing environments.

They are commonly used in factories to assemble products, move materials, weld components, and paint surfaces. Unlike robots operating in unpredictable outdoor environments, many industrial robots work in structured spaces where tasks and movements can be precisely controlled. Their main purpose is to improve manufacturing productivity, precision, and consistency.

by Emanuele Tonazzo

How Do They Work?

Industrial robots combine mechanical components, electronic systems, and specialized software.

  • Robotic arms: Move tools or objects using multiple joints and motors.
  • Controllers: Execute programmed instructions and coordinate movements.
  • Sensors: Monitor positions, forces, or environmental conditions.
  • Vision systems: Allow certain robots to identify and locate objects using cameras.

Many industrial robots perform predefined sequences of movements. More advanced systems can use sensor feedback to adjust their actions.


Real-World Example: ABB Robotics

ABB is a major manufacturer of industrial robotic systems used in automotive, electronics, andother manufacturing industries. Its robots can perform activities such as welding, painting, assembly, and material handling. For example, robotic arms can be programmed to weld components in a car factory, repeating the same operation with high precision. ABB also develops collaborative robots, known as cobots, which are designed to work alongside humans when appropriate safety requirements are met.


Benefits and Challenges

Industrial robots can perform repetitive tasks with high accuracy and consistency. They can improve production efficiency and reduce human exposure to certain hazardous working conditions. However, robotic systems can be expensive to install and maintain. They also require programming, technical expertise, and appropriate safety measures. Traditional industrial robots may need to be reprogrammed when manufacturing requirements change.


The Future

Industrial robotics is becoming more flexible through advances in artificial intelligence, sensors, and collaborative technology. Future factories may combine traditional automation with intelligent robots capable of adapting to different products and working more effectively alongside human employees.
