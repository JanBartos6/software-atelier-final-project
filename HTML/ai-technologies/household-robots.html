Robots at Home: The Rise of Robotic Vacuum Cleaners

Household robots are machines designed to assist people with everyday domestic tasks.

One of the most successful examples is the robotic vacuum cleaner, which automatically cleans floors inside homes. Robotic vacuum cleaners have become an important example of domestic robotics, with millions of units sold worldwide. However, developing a successful household robot is not only about making it perform its task correctly. It must also adapt to the environment and the needs of the people using it.

by Emanuele Tonazzo

How Do They Work?

Robotic vacuum cleaners use navigation systems to move through indoor environments and clean floors. Their performance depends on several important factors:

  • Navigation: The robot must move through the environment and cover the areas that need cleaning.
  • Mapping: Technologies that represent the surrounding environment can help improve navigation and energy efficiency.
  • Energy consumption: Different navigation technologies and design choices influence how much energy the robot uses.
  • Cleaning performance: The robot must clean effectively while operating within its technical limitations.

The choice of navigation technology can therefore influence both the robot's cleaning performance and its energy consumption.


Real-World Research: EPFL Study

Researchers at the École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne (EPFL) investigated how robotic vacuum cleaners perform in real households. Their research combined two different studies. In the first study, researchers analyzed seven robotic vacuum cleaners to understand how technologies such as navigation systems affect technical performance and energy consumption. In the second study, they observed the use of robotic vacuum cleaners in nine households to understand users' needs and how robots fit into everyday domestic routines. By combining technical evaluations with observations of real users, the researchers investigated both the performance of the robots and their integration into people's homes.


Benefits and Challenges

Robotic vacuum cleaners can help people perform routine cleaning tasks automatically. However, the EPFL research identified several important challenges. One challenge is energy efficiency. The way a robot navigates can influence its energy consumption and cleaning performance. Another challenge is user acceptance. A robot may work correctly from a technical perspective but still fail to meet the expectations of its users. The researchers found that users' needs and perceptions were not always sufficiently considered in robot design. Robots may also be rejected when they do not fit well into people's existing domestic environments and routines.


The Future of Household Robots

The EPFL study suggests that improving domestic robots requires more than developing better technology. Researchers and designers should consider both technical performance and human needs. Improvements in navigation, mapping, and energy efficiency could make robotic vacuum cleaners more effective. At the same time, understanding how people interact with robots is essential for creating machines that integrate successfully into everyday life.
