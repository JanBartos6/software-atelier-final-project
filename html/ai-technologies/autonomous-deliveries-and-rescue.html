Autonomous Deliveries and Rescue Robots

Robots can transport essential supplies and help people during emergencies.

Ground robots, delivery drones and rescue robots are being developed to operate in places where access can be difficult or dangerous.

by Fabio Giandomenico

Delivering food, packages and medical supplies

Robots are increasingly being used to deliver food, packages and medical supplies. There are two main types: ground delivery robots, which move along sidewalks, and aerial delivery drones, which transport packages through the air.

Starship Technologies, for example, developed small autonomous robots that use cameras, GPS and sensors to navigate sidewalks, avoid obstacles and deliver food and groceries.

Another important example is Zipline, a company that uses autonomous drones to deliver medical products. In 2016, Rwanda launched the world's first national drone delivery service with Zipline, initially focusing on transporting blood to hospitals, including facilities in remote areas. This technology helps healthcare services reach places that are difficult to access by road and can reduce delivery times.

Search and rescue robots

Robots can also play an important role during emergencies and natural disasters. Search and rescue robots are designed to enter dangerous environments, such as collapsed buildings and areas affected by earthquakes, fires or industrial accidents.

They can be equipped with cameras, thermal sensors and other instruments to detect survivors, explore unstable structures and provide information to rescue teams. Both ground robots and aerial drones can be used in these situations.

Their main advantage is that they can enter areas that may be too dangerous for human rescuers. This reduces risks and helps emergency services understand the situation before entering. The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) works on testing and developing performance standards for emergency response robots, focusing on mobility, navigation, sensing and reliability.

The challenges and the future

Despite their progress, robots operating in the open world still face several challenges. Unlike controlled environments, the real world is unpredictable: weather conditions can change, sensors may fail, GPS signals can be lost and unexpected obstacles can appear.

Robots must also understand human behaviour and make safe decisions in complex situations. Reliability is especially important: a robot used for medical deliveries or rescue operations must work correctly because mistakes can have serious consequences.

In the future, improvements in Artificial Intelligence, machine learning and sensor technology could make robots more autonomous, adaptable and capable of performing increasingly complex tasks. However, ensuring their safety and reliability will remain essential.
