World Models: Understanding the Physical World

How can artificial intelligence learn to understand the world around us?

Artificial intelligence has made enormous progress in understanding language, recognizing images, and generating digital content. However, interacting with the physical world presents a different challenge. A robot must do more than recognize objects: it needs to understand how they move, how they interact, and what might happen when it takes an action.

This is where world models become important.

by Giulia

What Is Physical AI?

Physical AI refers to artificial intelligence systems designed to perceive, understand, and interact with the physical world.

Unlike AI systems that primarily process digital information, physical AI can help robots and autonomous machines perform tasks in real environments.

Consider a robot working in a warehouse. To move a package, it must recognize the object, estimate its position, avoid obstacles, and control its movements. If a person suddenly walks in front of it, the robot must adapt its behavior.

These tasks require an understanding of an environment that is constantly changing.

What Is a World Model?

A world model is an AI system that learns a representation of an environment and can use it to predict how that environment might change.

World models can learn from different types of information, including images, videos, movement data, and actions. Some can also process text, sound, and 3D information.

For example, imagine a robot holding a glass above a table.

A world model could help it predict that releasing the glass would cause it to fall. Depending on the information the model has learned, it might also predict whether the glass would land safely or break.

The important idea is that the system is not simply recognizing a glass. It is attempting to understand what could happen next.

This ability to represent space, objects, and their relationships is closely connected to spatial intelligence.

Why Do Robots Need World Models?

Traditional robotics has often relied on mathematical models, carefully programmed instructions, and control algorithms.

These approaches remain extremely useful, especially in predictable environments where the robot's tasks and surroundings are well defined.

However, real environments are often unpredictable. Objects can be moved, lighting conditions can change, and people can behave unexpectedly.

Learning-based approaches offer another possibility: rather than relying exclusively on predefined rules, robots can learn useful patterns from data and experience.

World models can support this process by helping robots anticipate the consequences of different actions.

From Understanding to Acting

Imagine teaching a robot to pick up a cup.

First, the robot must recognize the cup and locate it in three-dimensional space. Then, it must decide how to approach the cup, position its gripper, and apply enough force to lift it without dropping it.

A predictive world model could help the robot estimate what might happen when it moves its gripper or changes its grip.

A robot that can predict the consequences of its actions may be able to plan and adapt more effectively.

But how do we create AI systems capable of making these predictions?

That begins with data, training, and learning how the world behaves.
