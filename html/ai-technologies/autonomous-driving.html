Autonomous Driving

How self-driving cars perceive the road and make decisions.

Self-driving vehicles combine cameras, LiDAR, radar and Artificial Intelligence to detect what is around them and navigate real streets.

by Fabio Giandomenico

How does a self-driving car see the road?

One of the most important applications of robotics in the open world is autonomous driving. Self-driving cars must understand a constantly changing environment that includes other vehicles, pedestrians, traffic lights and unexpected obstacles.

To collect information, these vehicles use different types of sensors:

  • Cameras capture images of the road, signs, traffic lights and other road users.

  • LiDAR uses laser pulses to measure distances and help create a three-dimensional view of the surroundings.

  • Radar detects objects and measures their distance and movement.

The role of Artificial Intelligence

Artificial Intelligence processes the information collected by the sensors. It helps the vehicle understand the environment, predict what other road users might do and choose a safe route. The system must continuously update its decisions as traffic conditions change.

Self-driving cars also combine sensor information with detailed maps. Maps provide useful information about the road network, while real-time data helps the vehicle react to the situation it is actually facing.

The example of Waymo

One of the best-known companies in this field is Waymo. It has developed autonomous vehicles capable of transporting passengers through real city streets without a human driver. Its vehicles use detailed maps and information collected by their sensors to navigate traffic and respond to unexpected situations.

Autonomous driving shows how robots can perform complex tasks outside controlled environments. At the same time, driving safely among people requires accurate perception, reliable decisions and careful attention to unexpected events.
