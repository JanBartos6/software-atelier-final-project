How Do World Models Work?

From real-world observations to AI-generated environments.

World models learn to represent environments by analyzing large amounts of data. Their purpose is not simply to generate realistic images, but to capture useful relationships between objects, actions, and changes over time.

To understand how these models are developed, we can divide the process into several stages.

by Giulia

1. Data Collection and Pre-processing

The first step is collecting information about the world.

Depending on the application, training data can include videos, images, 3D scenes, robot movements, sensor readings, and descriptions of actions.

For example, videos of people picking up objects can provide information about how hands move, how objects respond to contact, and how different actions change a scene.

Before training, the data must be prepared.

Data pre-processing involves organizing, cleaning, and filtering the information. Low-quality or duplicated samples may be removed, while particularly useful situations can be selected.

A good dataset should also contain a variety of environments and interactions, allowing the model to learn more than one specific situation.

2. Pre-training

During pre-training, the model learns general patterns from large datasets.

For example, by analyzing videos, it may learn that objects usually move continuously, that unsupported objects tend to fall, or that an object becomes partially hidden when another object passes in front of it.

The model adjusts its internal parameters to improve its predictions or reconstructions.

Different architectures use different training objectives. Some predict future video frames, while others learn compressed representations of their observations.

The objective is to build a useful understanding of how environments appear and evolve.

3. Specialized Training

After general training, a model can be adapted to a specific application.

For robotics, this might involve training with data collected from robot cameras, actions, and sensors.

For example, a model trained on general videos could be further adapted to understand the movement of a robotic arm.

This type of specialized training can improve performance on particular tasks, although not all world models follow the same training pipeline.

4. Post-training and Evaluation

The model can then be refined and evaluated to determine whether its predictions are useful.

Researchers may test how accurately it predicts object movements, how consistently it represents a 3D scene, or how well it responds to different actions.

For robotics, visual realism alone is not enough. A model also needs to represent the consequences of actions with sufficient accuracy for its intended use.

How Does a World Model Predict the Future?

Imagine that a robot is looking at a ball resting on a table.

The model receives information about the current situation. If it is conditioned on an action, such as pushing the ball, it can generate a prediction of what might happen next.

The predicted outcome may depend on the direction of the push, the position of the ball, and the physical relationships the model has learned.

Some world models predict future images or videos. Others predict future states in a compressed internal representation.

These predictions can help an AI system compare possible actions before performing them.

World Models vs. Physics Simulators

Although they are closely related, world models and traditional physics simulators are not the same thing.

A physics simulator calculates the behavior of objects using mathematical models of properties such as gravity, friction, and collisions.

A learned world model, on the other hand, uses patterns extracted from data to make predictions about an environment.

For example, a physics simulator might calculate how a ball falls using equations of motion. A learned world model might predict its movement from patterns observed during training.

Both methods have advantages and limitations. Physics simulators depend on the accuracy of their mathematical assumptions and parameters, while learned world models can produce incorrect or inconsistent predictions.

Combining learned world models with physics-based simulation is an important research direction.

From World Models to World Building

Once a system can represent or generate an environment, it may become possible to create entirely new virtual spaces for robots to explore.

Instead of manually constructing every room, obstacle, and object, developers can use generative AI to help create diverse training environments.

But creating a convincing virtual world is only the beginning.

The next step is using these environments to teach robots how to act.
