Robots in the Open World

Robots are moving beyond factories and entering the unpredictable world around us.

Artificial Intelligence, sensors and computer vision allow modern robots to interact with people, navigate unfamiliar places and make decisions independently.

by Fabio Giandomenico

From controlled environments to the real world

In the past, most industrial robots operated in controlled environments, such as factories. They performed repetitive tasks and followed predefined instructions, while the surrounding conditions remained largely predictable.

Modern robotics aims to develop machines capable of operating in the open world: real environments where conditions constantly change. Unlike factory robots, these machines must deal with unexpected obstacles, moving people, different weather conditions and unfamiliar places.

To understand what is happening around them, open-world robots use Artificial Intelligence, cameras, sensors, GPS and computer vision. These technologies help them collect information, interpret their surroundings and make decisions autonomously.

Where can we find these robots?

Robots operating in the open world are already used in several areas. Self-driving cars move through city streets, drones inspect fields and infrastructure, delivery robots transport food and packages, and rescue robots help emergency services during disasters.

  • Autonomous driving: cars use cameras, LiDAR, radar and AI to understand traffic and navigate roads.

  • Drones: flying robots support agriculture, inspections, environmental monitoring and search operations.

  • Autonomous deliveries: ground robots and aerial drones transport food, packages and medical supplies.

  • Search and rescue: robots explore dangerous areas and provide information to emergency teams.

A different kind of environment

The main difference between a factory and the open world is predictability. A factory robot can repeat the same movement in a space designed for it. A robot in a street, field or disaster area cannot know exactly what will happen next.

This makes autonomy especially important. The robot must continuously gather information, interpret changes and choose how to act. The following pages explore some of the most important applications: autonomous vehicles, drones, delivery systems and rescue robots.
