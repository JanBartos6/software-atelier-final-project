Voice-Based Language Technologies

The transition from typing to talking

The two types of voice based language technologies, and their union into voice assistants.

by Daniele Gabriele Gambarota

From typing to talking

Speaking is the most natural way humans communicate, so it is no surprise that companies wanted machines to understand our voice. Voice technologies solve two opposite problems: turning sound into text and turning text into sound.

Speech recognition (speech-to-text)

Automatic speech recognition (ASR) converts spoken words into written text. You meet it when you:

  • dictate a message instead of typing it;
  • see automatic subtitles on videos or live meetings;
  • give a voice command to your phone, TV or car.

Until the early 2010s, ASR relied on hand-built statistical models. The arrival of deep neural networks brought a big improvement in accuracy. More recent open systems, such as OpenAI’s Whisper, are trained on hundreds of thousands of hours of recordings in many languages and work reasonably well even with accents and noise.

Speech synthesis (text-to-speech)

Text-to-speech (TTS) does instead the reverse: it turns written text into a spoken voice. Everyday examples include GPS directions, audiobooks and screen readers for blind or visually impaired users. Older synthetic voices sounded robotic because they stitched together recorded fragments. Neural models such as WaveNet and Tacotron 2 generate the sound wave directly and produce voices much closer to natural human speech.

Voice assistants: merging text-to-speech and automatic speech recognition

Assistants like Siri, Alexa or Google Assistant chain several technologies in a "pipeline":

  1. Wake word detection: the device listens for a phrase such as “Hey Siri” and ignores everything else.
  2. Speech recognition: your voice is converted to text.
  3. Language understanding: the system works out what you want (“set a timer”, “play music”).
  4. Action and answer: it carries out the request or finds a reply.
  5. Speech synthesis: the reply is spoken aloud.
