Drones in the Open World

Flying robots can reach places that are difficult or dangerous for people.

Equipped with cameras, GPS and sensors, drones collect information and support activities ranging from agriculture to search and rescue.

by Fabio Giandomenico

What can drones do?

Drones are another important example of robots operating in the open world. They can fly autonomously or with limited human supervision and are used for several purposes, including agriculture, infrastructure inspection, environmental monitoring, photography and search and rescue operations.

They are equipped with cameras, GPS and other sensors. These technologies allow them to explore large or difficult-to-reach areas, collect information and identify potential problems.

Applications in different fields

  • Agriculture: drones can observe fields and collect information about crops.

  • Infrastructure inspection: they can examine structures and places that are difficult to access.

  • Environmental monitoring: they can gather information over large areas.

  • Photography: they can capture images and video from the air.

  • Search and rescue: they can help emergency services explore dangerous areas and locate missing people.

Helping during emergencies

During natural disasters, drones equipped with thermal cameras can help emergency services locate missing people. Thermal cameras detect heat, making it possible to identify people in situations where they may be difficult to see with ordinary cameras.

Drones can also assess dangerous areas without immediately putting rescuers at risk. They can reach places that are difficult to access and provide information that helps emergency teams understand the situation before entering.

These applications demonstrate why drones are useful in the open world: they can move through environments that are large, unfamiliar or potentially dangerous, while collecting information for people on the ground.
