Can AI Help Save the Environment?

Artificial Intelligence in Real Life · Page 4 of 4

How AI may help address environmental challenges, and what responsible use requires.

by LEXIE JOWI

4. Artificial intelligence as a solution to environmental problems

Although artificial intelligence creates environmental challenges, it also offers opportunities to address some of the world's most serious environmental problems. When used responsibly, AI can help researchers analyse large amounts of environmental information, identify patterns and make better decisions.

4.1 Monitoring climate change and environmental conditions

Scientists collect large quantities of information from satellites, sensors and weather stations. AI systems can process these datasets and identify changes in the environment, including:

  • Rising temperatures.
  • Deforestation.
  • Changing sea levels.
  • Variations in air quality.

A real-world example: UNEP's World Environment Situation Room. The UNEP uses its World Environment Situation Room (WESR) to organise and analyse environmental information. The platform uses digital technologies, including AI, to support monitoring indicators such as atmospheric carbon dioxide concentrations, glacier changes and sea-level rise. This can help governments and researchers understand environmental changes and respond to emerging problems more effectively.

4.2 Improving renewable energy systems

Renewable energy sources, such as solar and wind power, are important in reducing dependence on fossil fuels. However, their electricity production can vary depending on weather conditions.

AI can improve renewable energy management by:

  • Analysing weather forecasts.
  • Predicting electricity demand.
  • Estimating how much energy solar panels and wind turbines will produce.
  • Helping manage electricity distribution.

An AI-powered energy management system can predict periods of high electricity demand and help operators decide when to store excess renewable electricity or distribute it through the power grid. The IEA (2025) identifies AI as a technology that could improve the efficiency and operation of the energy sector, including electricity networks, energy production and renewable integration.

4.3 Protecting forests, wildlife and biodiversity

Satellite images, camera traps and environmental sensors can generate large amounts of information about forests and wildlife. AI tools can help analyse these images and identify changes that may indicate:

  • Illegal deforestation.
  • Habitat destruction.
  • Threats to animal populations.

UNEP has highlighted the use of AI in monitoring deforestation and environmental changes. These applications can help conservation organisations identify areas that require attention and make better-informed decisions about protecting natural resources.

4.4 Making AI more environmentally sustainable

The benefits of AI do not automatically cancel out the environmental costs of operating it. A technology may help reduce emissions in one industry while increasing electricity consumption through its own infrastructure. Sustainable AI therefore requires responsible development and use.

Who?What can they do?
Technology companiesInvest in renewable electricity and energy-efficient processors.
Data-centre operatorsReduce water consumption and improve cooling systems.
ManufacturersExtend hardware lifespan and improve recycling.
GovernmentsEncourage transparent environmental reporting and sustainable infrastructure.
Users and organisationsChoose appropriate AI tools and avoid unnecessary computing-intensive tasks.

Conclusion: Balancing progress with environmental responsibility

Artificial intelligence is becoming an increasingly important part of modern society. Its applications offer opportunities to improve healthcare, education, communication, scientific research and environmental protection. However, the technology also depends on resources that have environmental consequences.

The growing demand for electricity, water required for cooling, production of specialised hardware and generation of electronic waste show that AI is not entirely digital in its environmental impact. Behind every AI system is physical infrastructure that requires resources and responsible management.

At the same time, AI can support efforts to address climate change, protect biodiversity, monitor pollution and improve renewable energy systems. The challenge is to develop and operate AI in a way that minimises environmental harm. Technological progress and environmental sustainability should work together, rather than compete.

Ultimately, the future of artificial intelligence should not be measured only by how advanced or powerful the technology becomes. It should also be measured by how responsibly it uses the planet's resources.
