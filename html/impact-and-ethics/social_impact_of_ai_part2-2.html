AI in Everyday Life: Its Social Impact

Part 2 of 4: the positive side of AI

AI can make learning more personal, help doctors, give people with disabilities more independence and take over boring work. This part looks at the benefits in education, health, accessibility and work.

by Eleonora

2. The Positive Side

Education

AI can make learning more personal. In a normal class, the teacher has to go at one speed for everybody. A learning app can give an easier exercise to someone who struggles and a harder one to someone who is bored. Language apps can correct pronunciation immediately, and an AI tutor can explain a topic again at midnight without getting tired. This helps especially students whose families cannot pay for private lessons.

The risk is that students stop thinking for themselves. If somebody lets a chatbot do all the homework, they finish faster but learn very little. So the real question for schools is not whether students will use AI, because they already do, but how to teach them to use it as a help and not as a replacement for their own brain.

Health

In medicine, AI programs can look at X-rays or scans and help doctors notice signs of illness earlier. They are also used to speed up research for new medicines. Smartwatches track heart rate and sleep, which makes some people more aware of their health. In places with very few doctors, simple digital tools can at least offer basic advice and screening.

Still, a doctor is more than a machine that gives a diagnosis. Doctors talk to patients, understand the context and take responsibility. AI can make mistakes too, especially for patients who were not well represented in the data it learned from. That is why a human has to check the result.

Accessibility

For people with disabilities, AI can change daily life in a very concrete way. Voice control lets people who cannot use their hands operate a phone or computer. Automatic captions make videos and meetings easier to follow for deaf and hard-of-hearing people. Apps that describe a picture or read a sign aloud help people who are blind. Real-time translation also helps migrants who are still learning a new language. In all these cases the technology gives people more independence.

Work

AI can take over boring and repetitive tasks such as entering data, organising appointments or sorting documents. Then workers have more time for things that need creativity or contact with other people. It can also help small businesses and freelancers, who now have tools that once only big companies could afford.
