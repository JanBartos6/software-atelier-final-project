The Hidden Environmental Cost of AI

Artificial Intelligence in Real Life · Page 1 of 5

Exploring the hidden environmental costs of AI and its potential to protect our planet.

by LEXIE JOWI

1. Introduction: More than just technology

Artificial intelligence has become an important part of everyday life. From virtual assistants and recommendation systems to medical research, education and self-driving vehicles, AI is changing how people work, communicate and solve problems.

However, behind these convenient technologies is a physical infrastructure that requires significant natural resources. Although AI is often experienced through a screen, its operation depends on computers, data centres, electricity, water and raw materials.

What exactly is AI's environmental impact?

The environmental impact of artificial intelligence refers to the effects that developing, training and using AI systems have on the natural environment. These effects do not come from software alone. They also arise from:

  • Manufacturing computer chips.
  • Constructing and operating data centres.
  • Generating electricity.
  • Cooling computer equipment.
  • Disposing of outdated electronic devices.

According to the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP, 2024), the environmental consequences of AI must be examined throughout its entire life cycle. This includes the preparation of data, development and training of AI models, their everyday use, and the production and disposal of the hardware that supports them.

The bigger picture: Looking only at the electricity consumed when someone uses an AI application would give an incomplete picture of its environmental impact.

Why should we be concerned?

One reason this issue has become increasingly important is the rapid growth of AI technology. More individuals, businesses, schools and governments are adopting AI tools, while companies are developing increasingly powerful systems. These developments require larger computing facilities and more advanced equipment.

The International Energy Agency (IEA, 2025) explains that the expansion of AI is closely connected to growing electricity demand from data centres. This raises an important question: can society continue to benefit from increasingly advanced artificial intelligence without placing excessive pressure on the environment?

Is AI entirely bad for the environment?

Not necessarily. The relationship between AI and the environment is not entirely negative. AI can also help researchers understand environmental changes, improve renewable energy systems and monitor pollution. Understanding its impact requires examining both the resources it consumes and the solutions it can provide.
