The Energy Behind Artificial Intelligence

Artificial Intelligence in Real Life · Page 2 of 4

Energy consumption, data centres and carbon emissions.

by Lexie Jowi

2. Energy consumption and carbon emissions

2.1 Where does AI get its computing power?

Artificial intelligence systems require computing power to process information, identify patterns and produce results. This work is carried out using specialised computer processors, often located in large facilities known as data centres.

Data centres are buildings that contain servers, storage systems and networking equipment. They allow digital services to operate continuously, including cloud storage, online platforms and AI applications. When an AI model is trained, thousands of processors may work together to process enormous amounts of information. Even after training is complete, running the model for users requires additional computing resources.

415 TWhEstimated electricity consumed by data centres worldwide in 2024.
945 TWhProjected worldwide data-centre electricity consumption by 2030.

Source: International Energy Agency (2025).

The IEA's Energy and AI report (2025) estimated that data centres consumed approximately 415 terawatt-hours (TWh) of electricity in 2024, equivalent to around 1.5% of global electricity consumption. The agency projected that worldwide data-centre electricity consumption could reach approximately 945 TWh by 2030, with AI being a major driver of this growth.

Important: These figures refer to data centres as a whole, not AI alone. Data centres also support many other digital services. Nevertheless, the rapid development of AI is increasing demand for more powerful computing infrastructure.

2.2 The connection between electricity and carbon emissions

Electricity consumption does not automatically produce the same amount of carbon emissions everywhere. The environmental effect depends partly on how electricity is generated. In regions where electricity comes mainly from coal, oil or natural gas, operating data centres can contribute significantly to greenhouse gas emissions. These gases, particularly carbon dioxide, trap heat in the atmosphere and contribute to global warming.

How does this happen?

  1. An AI model requires electricity to perform calculations.
  2. Data centres supply the computing power needed to run the model.
  3. Electricity may be generated using fossil fuels.
  4. Burning fossil fuels releases greenhouse gases.
  5. These emissions contribute to climate change.

Training a large AI model can require substantial computing resources over an extended period. If the electricity used comes from fossil fuels, the training process can indirectly produce carbon emissions. Everyday use also contributes because AI models need electricity each time they generate responses, analyse information or perform other tasks.

2.3 Not all AI requests are equal

It is important not to assume that every AI request has the same environmental cost. A short text-based request and a complex video-generation task do not require equal computing resources. The type of task, complexity of the model and amount of computing required can all affect energy consumption.

2.4 The growing demand for data centres

As AI becomes more widely used, technology companies are investing in larger data centres and more advanced processors. This expansion can place additional pressure on electricity networks, particularly in areas where several large facilities are concentrated.

The IEA (2025) highlights that meeting future demand will require a combination of energy sources, including renewable energy, natural gas and nuclear power.

Why does this matter?

If AI infrastructure expands faster than clean electricity production, its environmental footprint could increase. Improving energy efficiency and increasing the use of renewable electricity are therefore essential steps towards making AI more sustainable.
